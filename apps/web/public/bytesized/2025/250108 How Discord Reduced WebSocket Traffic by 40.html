🔧 What Discord Needed To Solve

Handling real-time updates for millions of users is no small feat. Discord's gateway service delivers live updates, but this resulted in heavy bandwidth usage, particularly on mobile devices. The challenge?

  1. Reduce bandwidth without compromising real-time responsiveness.

  2. Improve compression while keeping memory and CPU usage under control.

  3. Handle evolving patterns in data efficiently.

🌐 Discord’s Two-Part Solution

1️⃣ Streaming Compression with Zstandard

  • Background: Since 2017, Discord used zlib for payload compression, but zstandard offered potential improvements in speed and compression ratios.

  • Initial Experiment: Early attempts with zstandard showed worse results due to lack of streaming compression, which enables reuse of previous data.

  • The Breakthrough: By modifying an open-source Elixir library, Discord enabled zstandard to support streaming, dramatically improving performance:

    • Payload sizes shrank from 270 bytes (zlib) to 166 bytes (zstandard).

    • Compression speed doubled, with zstandard taking 45 microseconds per byte compared to zlib’s 100.

2️⃣ Passive Sessions V2

  • What are Passive Sessions? For users not actively viewing a server, Discord sends periodic updates to keep their client in sync without overwhelming them with unnecessary data.

  • The Problem: The previous system sent full snapshots of server updates, even if only one element had changed.

  • The Fix: With Passive Sessions V2, Discord only sends delta updates (what changed), reducing passive session traffic from 35% to just 5% of total bandwidth.

🚀 Key Challenges and Solutions

  • False Starts with Zstandard: Initial experiments failed because Discord wasn’t using streaming compression. By adding support for this, they unlocked the real potential of zstandard.

  • Complexity of Dictionaries: While zstandard dictionaries promised marginal improvements, the added complexity of managing them across clients outweighed the benefits.

  • Memory Optimization: Efforts to optimize memory usage during off-peak hours faced challenges with memory fragmentation, highlighting the need to balance system intricacies with operational gains.

🌟 The Results

Through a combination of zstandard streaming and Passive Sessions V2, Discord slashed its gateway bandwidth usage by 40%. Here's how the improvements break down:

  1. Zstandard Streaming: Improved compression ratios and speeds across all platforms (desktop, iOS, and Android).

  2. Passive Sessions V2: Reduced redundant data transmission for inactive users, cutting traffic by 30%.

🧠 Advanced Insights: Engineering Learnings from Discord’s Optimizations

When it comes to large-scale systems, the devil is in the details. Discord’s journey to reduce WebSocket traffic by 40% offers a treasure trove of insights for senior engineers building distributed systems, optimizing compression algorithms, or solving high-scale data challenges. Let’s dive deeper into the engineering nuances that made this optimization possible and extract actionable takeaways for your own projects.

1. The Power of Streaming Compression

Streaming compression proved to be the turning point for Discord. Traditional compression algorithms work well for one-off tasks, but when dealing with long-lived connections—such as WebSockets—streaming compression becomes a game-changer. Here’s why:

  • Efficiency through Context Reuse: Streaming compression allows the algorithm to leverage previously seen data, creating a rolling context that improves efficiency. Without streaming, each payload starts fresh, losing the opportunity to build on past patterns.

  • Small Payloads, Big Impact: Discord’s payloads are small and frequent, making them the perfect candidate for streaming compression. If your system handles a similar workload, consider streaming options like zlib or zstandard.

  • Lesson: Not all workloads benefit from streaming. If your data is mostly unique or large (e.g., video streams), the benefits may not outweigh the added complexity.

Key Takeaway: Understand your data's size and patterns before deciding on a compression strategy. Streaming compression works best when payloads are frequent, small, and repetitive.

2. Balancing Trade-Offs in Compression Settings

Discord's engineers tuned zstandard’s parameters—like chainlog, hashlog, and windowlog—to strike a balance between compression speed, memory usage, and efficiency. These parameters directly impact your system’s performance:

  • Memory vs. Compression Ratio: Increasing parameters like chainlog improves compression but at the cost of higher memory usage. For instance, a larger chainlog gives the algorithm a longer history to reference, but this requires more memory.

  • Speed vs. Compression Ratio: Compression speed is critical in real-time systems. Discord settled on an optimal compression level of 6, balancing performance and memory overhead.

  • Lesson: Fine-tuning requires a deep understanding of both your hardware and workload. Over-optimizing one aspect (e.g., compression ratio) can lead to diminishing returns or unexpected bottlenecks.

Key Takeaway: Use profiling tools to evaluate the impact of compression settings on your workload. Start with defaults, then experiment incrementally to avoid overcomplicating your system.

3. When to Use Dictionaries in Compression

Zstandard’s dictionary support enables pre-seeding the algorithm with knowledge about the data it will compress, particularly useful for small payloads where there’s little context to learn from. However, Discord found this wasn’t worth the additional complexity. Why?

  • Gains vs. Overhead: For small payloads, dictionaries offer meaningful improvements (e.g., reducing “TYPING_START” payloads from 466 bytes to 187). However, managing dictionaries across a distributed system introduces complexity—both the server and client need synchronized dictionaries.

  • Evolving Data: As payload structures evolve, dictionaries become outdated, requiring frequent retraining and redeployment.

  • Lesson: Dictionaries shine in controlled environments or for static, repetitive data. In dynamic, high-scale systems like Discord, they may not justify the operational overhead.

Key Takeaway: Consider dictionaries for niche use cases, such as compressing static or highly predictable payloads. For dynamic systems, focus on robust streaming compression.

4. Delta Updates: Beyond Compression

Compression isn't always the best answer for reducing bandwidth. Discord’s transition from full snapshots to delta updates in Passive Sessions V2 highlights the importance of data design in optimization:

  • Avoid Redundant Data: Sending the same data repeatedly wastes bandwidth and CPU cycles. Delta updates ensure only the changed parts of a dataset are transmitted.

  • Lower CPU Costs: By reducing the volume of data, delta updates also decrease the computational overhead for encoding, compressing, and decoding.

  • Scalability at Scale: This approach made Discord’s infrastructure more scalable without requiring additional compute or memory resources.

Key Takeaway: Look for opportunities to reduce the size of transmitted data before focusing on compression. Efficient data design often yields bigger wins than algorithm tweaks.

5. Engineering with Feedback Loops

Discord’s use of feedback loops to dynamically allocate resources during off-peak hours is a masterclass in adaptive engineering:

  • Dynamic Scaling: By monitoring memory usage and adjusting zstandard buffers in real time, Discord optimized resource utilization without manual intervention.

  • Learning from Failure: Memory fragmentation in BEAM highlighted a blind spot. Although this experiment was reverted, the learnings informed future approaches.

  • Lesson: Feedback loops work best in systems with predictable patterns (e.g., diurnal traffic). However, they require careful instrumentation and robust monitoring to avoid overcorrections.

Key Takeaway: Build systems that adapt to real-world conditions. Feedback loops are invaluable for managing dynamic workloads but must be designed with clear guardrails to prevent unintended consequences.

6. Instrumentation

Discord’s ability to identify inefficiencies.like redundant Passive Session updates or memory fragmentation, came down to one thing: world-class instrumentation.

  • Granular Metrics: Tracking compression ratios, memory usage, and dispatch types allowed Discord to pinpoint bottlenecks and opportunities for improvement.

  • Visual Insights: Dashboards revealed patterns (e.g., passive session traffic consuming 35% of bandwidth) that might have gone unnoticed otherwise.

  • Lesson: Instrument everything. Without clear metrics, you're flying blind.

Key Takeaway: Invest in instrumentation before optimization. Tools like distributed tracing and real-time dashboards provide the visibility needed to make informed decisions.

7. Embrace Open-Source Contributions

Discord’s fork of the ezstd library to support streaming compression is a reminder of the power of open source. Instead of waiting for a solution, they created one, then contributed it back to the community.

Key Takeaway: Open source isn’t just about using tools, it’s about improving them. When your business depends on a technology, investing in its ecosystem benefits everyone.

Final Takeaways

For senior engineers tackling similar challenges, here’s what Discord’s journey teaches us:

  1. Know Your Data: The right optimization depends on understanding your data’s size, frequency, and patterns.

  2. Iterate Incrementally: Start with small experiments, like dark launches, to validate ideas before committing to large-scale changes.

  3. Balance Complexity: Every optimization has trade-offs. Always evaluate whether the gains outweigh the operational costs.

  4. Invest in Feedback Loops: Dynamically adapting to workload changes can unlock significant savings and improve system performance.

  5. Build for the Long Term: Optimize systems with scalability and maintainability in mind. Short-term wins aren’t worth future headaches.
